Part 3 — Mechanism-to-morphology alignment

Two candidate mechanisms -- local mechanical displacement and long-range spatial heterogeneity -- are added to the Part 2 cellular automaton and scored by the same morphological pipeline, to find which one reproduces the measured malignant signature (few large lobes).

Publish with

publish('scripts/report.m', struct('format','html','outputDir','results/report'))

Part 2 ended in a clean negative: an empty-neighbour lattice CA, however its four knobs are swept, yields near-circular Eden clusters (distance to the melanoma median ~1.15), because a surrounded cell has no empty site to divide into. The handoff named two concrete gaps -- displace neighbours (off-lattice / mechanical) or impose long-range spatial heterogeneity -- and this part implements both, compares them, and aligns the winner.

2. What the two new mechanisms do

Both extend Part 2's CA at the proliferation rule (ca3.step_proliferate):

  • Mechanical displacement -- a surrounded (layer-2) cell that passes the division test no longer gives up: it pushes one occupied neighbour into an adjacent empty site and divides into the vacated slot (O(1) local push). This lifts the "no empty neighbour" bottleneck so the interior can grow too.
  • Spatial heterogeneity -- a smooth static growth-rate field g(x,y) (sum of low-order Fourier modes, mean 1, correlation length lambda) multiplies each cell's division rate. A few broad growth-rate patches leave a few large lobes.

Both knobs are pre-registered; the data, not the author, picks the winner.

5. Phase B — OAT screening

One-at-a-time screening of the winning arm's two knobs (growth-field correlation length lambda and amplitude gAmp), 5 levels x 3 seeds each.

oatPath = fullfile(res, 'oat_spatial_heterogeneity.csv');
if isfile(oatPath)
    O = readtable(oatPath, 'TextType', 'string');
    base = mean(O.dist(strcmp(O.knob, 'baseline')), 'omitnan');
    fprintf('OAT baseline dist = %.3f\n', base);
    knobs = {'heteroAmp', 'heteroLambda'};
    for ki = 1:numel(knobs)
        idx = strcmp(O.knob, knobs{ki});
        lvls = unique(O.level(idx));
        mx = 0; bestLv = 0;
        for lv = lvls'
            d = mean(O.dist(idx & O.level == lv), 'omitnan');
            if abs(d - base) > mx, mx = abs(d - base); bestLv = lv; end
        end
        fprintf('  %-14s max |Delta dist| = %.3f (level %d)\n', knobs{ki}, mx, bestLv);
    end
end

9. Conclusions and limits

The growth-field mechanism reproduces the malignant signature that Part 2 could not: a few large lobes (low solidity, low convexity, high compactness) at near-constant D_f, landing within dist ~0.3-0.4 of the melanoma median -- roughly a third of Part 2's ~1.15 baseline. Local mechanical displacement does not: it still converges to a near-circle (dist ~1.13), confirming the pre-registered expectation that an incompressible pushed droplet relaxes to its minimal-energy round shape.

Limits carried into the handoff: (i) the growth field is an imposed smooth heterogeneity, not an emergent instability -- Part 4 must ask whether the correlated patches can arise from cell-autonomous dynamics; (ii) clean CA masks carry no segmentation noise, so absolute descriptor values differ from the real Otsu medians; (iii) the alignment is judged on the three primary descriptors only, with D_f reported secondarily.
